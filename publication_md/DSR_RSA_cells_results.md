# Results — DSR RSA across single-unit ROIs

This summary refers to the DSR comparison run saved in `DSR_RSA_simple_ROI/2026-06-22_16-17-15-DSR`. The analysis phase-residualised each cell's firing-rate trace with a cosine basis before RSA and compared three matched combo models that differed only in the DSR regressor: `ctrl_dsrFULL` (`dsr_fmri`), `ctrl_dsrFUT` (`dsr_fmri_fut`), and `fdr_dsrInformed` (`dsr_fmri_informed`). All three were fit jointly with `bttn_curr`, `bttn_next`, `location`, `l2_norm`, and `state`. The confirmatory test specified in the run configuration was `split_halves_z` for the `dsr_fmri_informed` regressor.

Under the confirmatory model (`fdr_dsrInformed`), HC_anterior showed the clearest DSR signal in the primary `split_halves_z` analysis (beta = 0.0599, t = 3.87, p_perm = 0.001998, q_FDR = 0.01399 across seven ROIs). No other ROI survived the pre-specified across-ROI FDR correction in that confirmatory family, although HC_mid (beta = 0.0303, p_perm = 0.03596), ACC (beta = 0.0304, p_perm = 0.03996), and Parahippocampal cortex (beta = 0.0280, p_perm = 0.06094) all showed positive tendencies. This is the right place to state the ACC a priori prediction carefully: the RSA does not establish ACC as the strongest or most selective DSR locus, but the sign and magnitude of the ACC effect are consistent with the broader hypothesis that ACC carries prospective task information. In that sense, the RSA results provide trend-level support for the idea, already motivated by other analyses, that ACC preferentially represents early future actions.

The two control DSR variants showed a different anatomical emphasis. In `split_halves_z`, both the full model (`ctrl_dsrFULL`) and the lag-0-dropped control (`ctrl_dsrFUT`) produced their strongest effects in HC_mid (beta = 0.0718 and 0.0672 respectively; both p_perm = 0.001998, q_FDR = 0.01399 if one applies BH-FDR across the seven ROIs within that combo). ACC was again positive in both models (beta = 0.0402 for `dsr_fmri`, beta = 0.0376 for `dsr_fmri_fut`) and nominally significant at the permutation level before across-ROI correction (both p_perm = 0.03097), which is compatible with the intended ACC framing but still weaker than the hippocampal effects. This indicates that the full and future-only DSR models preferentially load onto HC_mid, whereas the pre-registered informed model shifts the peak toward HC_anterior.

The secondary `between_tasks_z` analysis further sharpened this dissociation. For `ctrl_dsrFULL` and `ctrl_dsrFUT`, HC_mid again carried the only FDR-surviving effect (beta = 0.0637 and 0.0597; both p_perm = 0.00699, q_FDR = 0.04895). By contrast, for `fdr_dsrInformed`, the largest between-task effect moved to Parahippocampal cortex (beta = 0.0595, t = 3.62, p_perm = 0.001998, q_FDR = 0.01399 in an exploratory within-combo BH correction), whereas HC_anterior showed only a nominal effect (beta = 0.0316, p_perm = 0.04695) and HC_mid showed none. Thus, the three DSR model instantiations were not interchangeable: the full and future-only variants tracked a hippocampal-midbody signal, while the informed lag-1/lag-2 model expressed its strongest effects in anterior hippocampus in the confirmatory cross-run test and in parahippocampal cortex in the secondary between-task test.

Overall, the DSR analysis does not support a uniform whole-brain action-plan code. Instead, the topography depends on which temporal-lag instantiation of the DSR model is entered into the control stack. The pre-registered confirmatory claim is therefore specific: after phase residualisation and control for button, location, and state structure, `dsr_fmri_informed` was significant in HC_anterior in the primary `split_halves_z` analysis. For ACC, the fairest summary is more qualified: RSA effects are positive and in the predicted direction across several DSR specifications, consistent with an a priori prospective-action hypothesis, but they remain trend-level relative to the stronger hippocampal and parahippocampal signals.
